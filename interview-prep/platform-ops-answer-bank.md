冲突解决
Situation
Conflict between legal team (strict screening) and operations team (concerned about user experience and complaint surge).
Legal wanted to block all suspected sensitive words, while operations feared overblocking would harm user experience.
Task
Find a solution that balanced compliance and user experience.
Action
Analyzed 3 months of violation data → found <20% were truly risky, while ~80% were vague words (not clearly violating, just uncomfortable).
Facilitated a cross-team meeting and showed that “one-size-fits-all blocking” would lead to wrongly filtering normal content (e.g. “bad customer service attitude”).
Proposed a layered solution:
Must-block words → trigger immediate blocking (100% compliance).
To-be-reviewed words → sent to manual secondary review, providing buffer for misjudgment cases.
Ran a small-scale trial in one business line for 2 weeks. Every day, we shared the blocking data and user feedback:
Legal team: satisfied with 100% blocking rate for high-risk terms.
Operations team: saw complaint rate drop by 20% for misjudged content.
Gradually, both teams accepted the solution.
Result
After full rollout, overall complaint rate dropped by 15%.
Successfully balanced legal compliance and user experience.

封面治理
S – Situation
We saw a large amount of low-quality cover images in the dual-column feed. These didn’t violate policy but significantly hurt user experience and recommendation efficiency. Data showed low-quality covers accounted for 9% of external exposure, exceeding the platform’s risk threshold, and moderation also suffered from high false positives.
T – Task
My goal was to design a scalable governance mechanism that reduced low-quality exposure and false positives, without increasing manual moderation cost.
A – Action
I treated this as a platform problem:
Defined clear, structured criteria for low-quality visuals and translated them into system rules.
Enabled data and model improvements using curated samples.
Designed a tiered moderation workflow, combining model confidence with human review.
Added exception handling for borderline cases like “tearful” content.
R – Result
Low-quality external exposure dropped from 9% to 3.8%.
False positives decreased by 76%, reducing 380 manual reviews per day.
Overall moderation efficiency improved by 18%.
Summary: This showed me that governance works best when ambiguity is handled through systems, not manual rules.

场景题 1：排查问题
某个海外市场的 PGC campaign 表现明显低于预期，你会怎么排查和处理？
中文回答
结论：
我会从目标、机制、执行三层系统排查，先判断问题类型，再给对应解法。
拆解思路：
第一层看目标和数据，判断是供给不足、参与度低，还是转化效率问题。
第二层看机制，检查门槛、激励和创作指引是否清晰，是否符合当地创作者能力和内容习惯。
第三层看执行，确认 SOP 是否被正确理解和落地，比如配置是否一致、指引是否到位。
动作：
如果是执行问题，用 checklist 和模板快速纠偏。
如果是机制问题，先在小市场或子人群做验证，再推广到其他市场。
English Answer
Conclusion:
I would diagnose the issue across goals, mechanics, and execution, then apply targeted fixes.
Breakdown:
First, I look at metrics to see whether the issue is supply, participation, or conversion.
Second, I review campaign mechanics, including thresholds, incentives, and creator guidelines, and whether they fit local creator capabilities.
Third, I check execution, such as SOP understanding, configuration consistency, and guidance delivery.
Action:
For execution issues, I fix them quickly with checklists and templates.
For mechanism issues, I test adjustments in a smaller market before scaling.

追问 1
面试官：
你说会从目标、机制、执行三层拆，那你最先看哪一个？为什么？
中文回答：
我会先看目标对应的核心指标。
因为如果目标本身不清晰，比如是拉供给还是提转化，后面的机制设计和执行动作很容易跑偏。
在确认目标之后，我才会判断机制是否真正支撑这个目标，最后再看执行是否到位。
English Answer:
I would start with the core metrics tied to the goal.
If the goal is unclear, for example whether we are driving supply or improving conversion, both the mechanism and execution can easily go in the wrong direction.
Once the goal is clear, I evaluate whether the mechanics support it, and only then look at execution.

This includes whether the entry bar is too high, whether the guidelines are too abstract, and whether creators clearly understand what success looks like.
If many creators drop off at the same step, it usually indicates a mechanism issue rather than poor execution.
